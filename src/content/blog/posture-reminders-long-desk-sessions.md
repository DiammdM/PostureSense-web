Deep focus changes what you notice. During a long desk session, attention stays with the document, code, or assignment while small changes in body position fade into the background. A posture reminder can bring that information back into awareness.

## Reminders create a moment to check in

A useful reminder does not need to tell you that your posture is objectively wrong. It can simply mark that something changed. You can then decide whether to sit back, move the screen, adjust the task, or ignore the cue because the current position is comfortable.

That distinction matters. Bodies move, and a single fixed pose is not a realistic goal for most people.

## Event-based and time-based reminders work differently

A time-based reminder appears on a schedule. It can be effective for regular movement breaks, but it does not know whether your posture changed.

An event-based posture reminder uses a signal—such as visible head or upper-body movement—to decide when a cue may be relevant. Posturely uses on-device camera processing during an active session to look for changes such as head-down posture, forward leaning, and body tilt.

## The quality of the cue matters

If every small movement produces an alert, reminders can become noise. If the threshold is too high, you may rarely receive one. Adjustable sensitivity helps you find a middle ground for your device position, normal movement, and type of work.

Sound is useful when the phone is not directly in view. Vibration can be more discreet in a quiet room. The better option depends on your environment and whether the device stand lets you feel the haptic cue.

## Pair reminders with a repeatable setup

Event-based detection depends on a stable reference. Place the phone on a stand where the camera can see your upper body from a consistent side-front angle. Avoid moving the stand after the session begins.

Lighting and obstructions also matter. A clear view makes it easier for the app to distinguish your movement from a changing camera setup.

## Use the reminder to support autonomy

The most sustainable posture habit is one you can adapt. Treat a reminder as information, not a judgment. Sometimes the right response is to adjust your chair. Sometimes it is to stand, stretch, or finish the current sentence first.

Posture reminders can support everyday awareness, but they are not medical diagnosis or treatment. If you have persistent pain or a health concern, consult an appropriate professional.
